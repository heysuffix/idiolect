The situation regarding EU energy policy has shifted considerably over the past quarter. Three separate reports, reviewed independently, point to the same conclusion: supply chain diversification is proceeding slower than projected.

The numbers tell a straightforward story. Import dependency dropped 8% year-on-year, against a target of 14%. Infrastructure investment reached €2.3 billion in Q3, up from €1.9 billion in Q2. These are not catastrophic figures, but they indicate a consistent gap between stated targets and actual progress.

Officials I spoke with — two from the Commission, one from a member state energy ministry — were reluctant to characterize this as a failure. The preferred framing was "adjustment." One used the phrase "calibrated timeline." I have noted this language in previous briefings on different topics.

What matters for the next six months: whether the revised targets in the December framework document will be reflected in actual procurement. That document is expected by November 15th. If it slips past December, the Q1 numbers will make the conversation significantly harder.

Context: this analysis draws on public procurement data, three industry reports, and four background conversations. No single source was treated as definitive.
